Episode 001: Protect the Margin
The Beautiful Margin

This is The Beautiful Margin, a private briefing about earning wisely, reacting less, and protecting the life the work is meant to serve.

This week's central idea is simple: you do not have an opportunity problem. You have a margin-allocation problem.

Over the past several weeks, professional outreach has begun to convert into real optionality. Several independent medical assessment and file-review channels are moving from conversation to onboarding. A new physician services agreement has been completed. Other organizations are discussing referrals, report work, disability assessment, occupational medicine, and related services.

At the same time, August is already spoken for in the ways that matter. The boys have camps and activities. Tennis registration opens early one morning. Your birthday is coming. There is family logistics, work, call, and a Vancouver Art Gallery day near the end of the month.

That calendar is not an obstacle to the business plan. It is the business plan's purpose.

The risk now is not that you will miss every opportunity. The risk is that each new opportunity will arrive wearing the costume of urgency, and that a collection of individually reasonable commitments will quietly consume the space you were trying to create.

So the first move is to price work in the currency that actually matters: collected, after-tax dollars per attention-hour.

An attention-hour is not merely sixty minutes on a clock. It includes the record review, the context switching, the email chain, the scheduling, the uncertainty about payment, the emotional residue, the drive, and the time required to become fully present again afterward.

A two-hour file review completed quietly at home may be worth more than a two-hour live assessment that also demands travel, coordination, a meeting, report revisions, and a disrupted evening. A nominal fee can look excellent while the attention economics are poor.

Before referrals begin to arrive in volume, set a ninety-day capacity rule.

First, protect the work that preserves your professional standing and dependable income.

Second, treat the shared family calendar as a hard boundary, especially the parts that involve Anna and the boys.

Third, allocate a small, fixed number of weekly blocks to the highest-value asynchronous work. Do not let the number of available referral channels determine the number of blocks. Let the life you want determine the cap.

For each channel, track five things at minimum: collected dollars per attention-hour, average time to payment, revision burden, scheduling burden, and fit with family life. Add travel, privacy friction, cancellation protection, and reputational risk if they materially differ.

The point is not to build a beautiful spreadsheet. The point is to make it easy to say yes to the right file and no to the wrong form of busyness.

Now for the unknown unknown.

Some of the medical work you are considering may not be GST-exempt health care.

The Canada Revenue Agency's policy on examinations, assessments, reports, and certificates focuses on the purpose of the service. If the purpose is protecting, maintaining, or restoring a person's health, the service may qualify as exempt health care. If the purpose is helping an employer, insurer, lawyer, or other party make a decision, the work can be taxable even when a physician performs it.

The CRA gives employment medical examinations as a clear example of work that is not a qualifying health-care supply. The current small-supplier threshold is thirty thousand dollars of taxable supplies, calculated under CRA rules. If taxable revenue exceeds the threshold in a single quarter, registration and charging obligations can begin with the transaction that crosses it.

This does not mean that every IME or report is taxable. It means the invoice classification should be designed now, before several referral streams begin producing work.

The useful question for an accountant is:

Which of my IME, disability, occupational, employer, insurer, file-review, and medico-legal services are taxable, when would the small-supplier threshold apply, and should my bookkeeping separate taxable and exempt services from the first invoice?

Ask that question in writing. Request a written answer. Then create the revenue categories before the first busy quarter.

There is a second tax issue nearby. A qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporation can currently face a nine percent federal small-business rate and a two percent British Columbia rate on eligible active business income within the applicable business limit. But that does not mean incorporation magically converts all earnings into an eleven percent final tax bill.

The advantage is often a deferral on money that stays inside the corporation. Salary, dividends, passive income, associated corporations, the amount you actually need for family spending, and professional-corporation rules all matter.

There is also a personal services business question when an incorporated professional would otherwise look like an employee of one payer. Your emerging mix of clients may be helpful context, but the answer depends on the actual working relationships and contracts.

The calm move is not to incorporate because the headline rate sounds attractive. It is to ask an accountant and a lawyer to model the decision around expected retained earnings, the mix of clients, GST, and the College's health profession corporation rules.

Now, the tool watch.

Doctors of BC has expanded its practical guidance and support for AI scribes. It links to readiness tools, privacy guidance, pre-qualified options, and trial programs. Recent randomized and systematic-review evidence suggests that ambient scribes can reduce documentation burden for some physicians. The same evidence base also shows variable adoption, variable quality, and the continued need for careful review.

This is relevant because you are already discussing clinic workflow tools. The best next step is a controlled test, not a campaign.

If a clinic wants to consider an AI scribe, use an approved system. Complete the privacy and consent work. Start with a small group. Measure after-hours charting and correction time. Review every note. The College is clear that the physician remains responsible for errors.

At work, offer the useful information once. If a demonstration would help the people who actually own the workflow, make that option available. Then stop. Do not become the unpaid internal champion, the critic of everyone who moves slowly, or the person who escalates a tool preference into a political question.

The tool is supposed to give time back. If advocating for the tool consumes political capital, meetings, and attention, the implementation has already contradicted its purpose.

For the IME business, automate the safest layer first. Build intake checklists, fee tables, scheduling rules, invoice tracking, turnaround alerts, report templates, and a source-citation method. Do not make claimant records the first experimental AI workflow. Protected information belongs only in systems that have passed the relevant privacy, security, contractual, and professional review.

Here is the work shield for this week.

Do not get recruited into an argument merely because someone offers you a role in it.

Before replying, ask four questions.

Is there a patient-safety, legal, ethical, privacy, harassment, or material employment issue?

Is action actually mine to take?

What is the lowest-energy factual move that could resolve it?

Can I write one neutral message and then stop?

When the answer is yes, use a small pattern: state the observable fact, state the practical effect, make one clear request.

Do not diagnose motives. Do not use sarcasm. Do not copy extra people to create pressure. Do not continue an asynchronous argument after the useful question is answered.

If there is a real safety duty, legal duty, privacy breach, serious harassment, fraud, threat, or unresolved contract issue, use the proper formal channel clearly. Non-escalation is not silence. It is the refusal to create heat when a smaller, proper action will do.

Your employment is an asset. Calm is a form of capital. Quiet usefulness compounds.

Finally, the beauty move.

The shared calendar already contains a Vancouver Art Gallery day on August thirtieth. Protect it from becoming an errand attached to the end of a workweek.

Try one small ritual. Each person chooses one work in the gallery. No one has to prove that the choice is sophisticated. At the end, each person describes what held their attention and why. The point is not art education. It is learning what the people you love notice.

For Anna, do not turn appreciation into another scheduled improvement program. Offer one specific sentence this week about something she carried for the family that you noticed. Specific gratitude is more credible than a general declaration, and current relationship research continues to connect gratitude and shared coping with relationship satisfaction.

Three moves for the week:

First, send one written question to the accountant about GST classification, the thirty-thousand-dollar threshold, professional incorporation, and personal services business exposure.

Second, set the ninety-day weekly cap for IME and file-review blocks before referrals arrive. Score each channel by collected dollars per attention-hour.

Third, protect the art gallery day and tell Anna one specific thing you noticed and appreciated.

That is enough.

The goal is not to fit more into the margin. The goal is to keep the margin beautiful.
